Readings are assigned by day and should be completed **before** the corresponding day of the intensive course. Each day lists a small **required** reading (marked *Skim* or *Read*); **optional** items are provided for those who want to go deeper.

::: {.callout-note}

## Access

PDFs for readings not freely available online are posted on **Google Classroom**. Where a work is open access, a DOI link is provided below.
:::

## Day 1 · Introduction & First Experience

**Skim**

- Abdurahman, S., Ziabari, A. S., Moore, A. K., Bartels, D. M., & Dehghani, M. (2025). A primer for evaluating large language models in social-science research. *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science, 8*(2). [https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459251325174](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459251325174)
  - Get a feel for the questions the course tackles; don't worry about the details yet.

## Day 2 · Annotation, Gold Standards & Metrics

**Read**

- Eguchi, M., & Kyle, K. (2024). Building custom NLP tools to annotate discourse-functional features for second language writing research: A tutorial. *Research Methods in Applied Linguistics, 3*(3), 100153. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100153](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100153)

**Skim §3.1–§3.2 before Session 5** — this is the corpus you annotate against all week, and Session 5 opens by asking you to extract two of its design decisions.

- Arase, Y., Uchida, S., & Kajiwara, T. (2022). CEFR-based sentence difficulty annotation and assessment. *Proceedings of the 2022 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing* (pp. 6206–6219). [https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.416](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.416)
  - Their Table 6 is the supervised benchmark your Day-3 prompt gets compared against in Session 9.

**Optional / further reading**

- Fuoli, M. (2018). A stepwise method for annotating appraisal. *Functions of Language, 25*(2), 229–258. [https://doi.org/10.1075/fol.15016.fuo](https://doi.org/10.1075/fol.15016.fuo)
  - The source of the three annotation principles used in Session 4; useful if you are building a scheme of your own.
- Kyle, K., & Eguchi, M. (2024). Evaluating NLP models with written and spoken L2 samples. *Research Methods in Applied Linguistics, 3*(2), 100120. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100120](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100120)
- Mizumoto, A. (2025). Automated analysis of common errors in L2 learner production: Prototype web application development. *Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, 1–18. [https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263125100934](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263125100934)
- Yamashita, T. (2024). An application of many-facet Rasch measurement to evaluate automated essay scoring: A case of ChatGPT-4.0. *Research Methods in Applied Linguistics, 3*(3), 100133. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100133](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100133)

## Citing the datasets

Your report must cite the corpus your track uses, not just the course. Full provenance,
licences and the paper for every track — CEFR-SP, RAAMove, CaRS-50, AutoErrorAnalyzer,
ICNALE GRA — is in [`resources/datasets/SOURCES.md`](../resources/datasets/index.md).
